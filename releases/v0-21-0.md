# Organon v0.21.0 — Identity, Missingness, and explicit Set

*Prepared October 13, 2026. Implemented in the review branch; publication awaits merge. No package or deployment is asserted by this note.*

The binding ontology registers **Identity** as exact logical sameness within Presence, and makes Difference its explicit complement. Missingness now names the specified represented or expected target and excludes any contained item identical to it. This is not a new logic or an empirical identity detector.

## Compatibility

- D120 (Identity), D121 (Set) and D122 (mathematical Order) are new. Existing term and claim identifiers are unchanged. There are 118 active terms, 115 definitions and 53 commitments. D046 keeps `organon:Order` under the label Coordination Order; mathematical Order uses `organon:MathematicalOrder`. Old anchors and font slots retain their meanings.
- Identity follows Presence; A5, D002 and D014 record the relevant new dependencies. The Absence dependencies of the revised foundational entries are exclusion references, not relations with Absence.
- The generic Lean Missingness record, constructors and existing preservation contracts keep their types. A new theorem proves that its prior nonmembership condition and the clarified identity-based condition are equivalent for one exact field and target.
- Existing private-use glyph slots retain their mappings. A new term's canonical position does not reassign font slots. Glyphs remain provisional and do not change ontology semantics.

## Verification and limits

The new formal module proves equality laws, a classical exclusive partition, and exact-target noncontainment. Finite witnesses distinguish matching appearance from Identity, nonempty wrong-target fields from fulfillment, target substitution, empty-field ambiguity, and ordered configurations from unordered membership.

These proofs do not establish that a physical field actually represents or expects the supplied target, identify real Entities, or produce a complete universe of Presence. The difference between a specific target and any member of a class is explicit. A fictional description does not establish its referent's existence. Existing canonical Entity-individuation limits are not claimed solved.

The [promotion dossier](../proposals/identity-and-missingness.md), [typed contracts](../proposals/identity-and-missingness-claims.json), and [integration review](../reviews/promotion/identity-structured-configuration.json) record the semantic challenge and verification boundaries. The [prior exact-source review](../reviews/promotion/identity-and-missingness.json) remains historical. Passing CI is local verification, not philosophical completeness.

# Long-Form Editorial Grammar

Companion model of recurring entities and relations: [Daniel's Ontology](./Daniels-Ontology.md).

> [!note] Working status
> Daniel has adopted the distinction between long-form preparation and short-form delivery. This document is the provisional grammar built from that decision. It should become canonical only after it survives use and revision across the long-form sample families.

## Founding proposition

The short-form language defines the **delivery**: the sentence length, argument shape, rhythm, precision, and flavor through which an idea becomes concrete.

Long-form writing defines the **relationship that makes the delivery possible**.

An article does not merely contain an argument. It creates the conditions under which a reader can perceive why the argument was missing, develop a use for it, and receive it as something worth carrying forward.

In Daniel's formulation:

> Each article mints a new frame of reference from which to see the world.

The minted frame is not merely a conclusion. It is a conceptual instrument the reader can use somewhere else.

## The social grammar of an article

Long-form structures are neither timeless laws nor arbitrary preferences. Readers learn forms socially: how an essay welcomes them, what counts as evidence, how long a writer may delay the thesis, when a surprise feels earned, and what kind of ending constitutes completion. These expectations vary across language, culture, medium, audience, and decade. Some underlying work remains.

The writer must establish a relationship, reveal consequential Missingness, negotiate resistance, deliver a usable idea, and return the reader to the world with a changed capacity to perceive it.

Here **Missingness** is an explicit editorial projection of the ontological term. The field is the reader's current frame; the expected Presence is an explanation, distinction, or usable instrument that the frame represents as needed; the omission is the frame's failure to contain it. If those three elements cannot be identified, the grammar should say `gap`, `problem`, or `unknown` rather than borrowing Missingness as atmosphere.

The grammar describes those forces. It does not prescribe visible sections.

## The central movement

```text
recognized world
    -> consequential missingness
        -> negotiated passage
            -> delivery
                -> revalued world
                    -> portable inheritance
```

The article begins in a world available to the reader. It ends in substantially the same world, but the reader now possesses a distinction that makes some part of it newly legible.

## The six functions

### 1. Hospitality

Establish enough shared reality for the reader to enter.

Hospitality does not require warmth. It may take the form of an ordinary object, a familiar frustration, a mystery, a technical invariant, an admission, or a direct confrontation. Its promise is simply: *I know where you are standing, and I can show you where we are going from here.*

Useful questions:

- What does the reader already recognize without accepting my theory?
- What concrete object, event, or situation can hold our shared attention?
- What have I earned the right to assume?

### 2. Disturbance

Reveal the Missingness the article exists to fill.

The disturbance is not merely the topic. It is the point at which the reader's existing frame stops accounting for something that matters. A deployment is green and the feature is absent. The official architecture cannot explain how work actually happens. A prompt describes a boundary the environment does not enforce.

The article becomes desirable when the reader can feel the cost of continuing without it.

Useful questions:

- What cannot the existing frame explain, distinguish, or permit us to do?
- Who experiences the consequence of that failure?
- Would the article still feel necessary if its thesis were removed?

### 3. Negotiation

Earn the reader's movement from the familiar frame toward the proposed one.

Examples, mechanisms, objections, failed explanations, counterexamples, history, and analogy all belong here. The writer is not merely accumulating support. The writer is discovering what the reader must surrender, preserve, or reinterpret before the delivery can land.

Negotiation should expose real resistance. If every example agrees immediately, the article is probably presenting rather than thinking.

Useful questions:

- What reasonable objection would prevent the reader from following?
- Which part of the old frame remains useful?
- What did I initially misunderstand?
- Where does my preferred metaphor stop mapping cleanly?

### 4. Delivery

Materialize the distinction, argument, image, or concept the preceding movement has made usable.

A delivery changes the classification of the problem. It does not merely summarize or intensify what the opening already said. The strongest delivery often feels surprising one sentence before it arrives and inevitable one sentence afterward.

The canonical [Short Form](./Samples/Short-Form.md) document governs the delivery's sentence-level behavior: compression, precision, irony, rhythm, conceptual turn, and stopping point.

Useful questions:

- What can the reader name or distinguish now that they could not name before?
- Does the sentence preserve the actors, mechanism, and stakes?
- Did the article earn this scale of claim?
- Can the delivery stop without a paragraph explaining its importance?

### 5. Revaluation

Return to the original world and let the new frame act upon it.

The spreadsheet is no longer an embarrassing exception; it is evidence of reality excluded by the canonical system. Completion is no longer a statement emitted by the actor; it is a relationship between a claim and an independent witness. The same object remains, but its meaning and possible uses have changed.

Revaluation is not a recap. It demonstrates the instrument.

Useful questions:

- What becomes visible when the new distinction is applied to the opening situation?
- Which decision, responsibility, or possibility changes?
- What becomes harder to pretend not to know?

### 6. Inheritance

Leave the reader with something that can live beyond the article.

An inherited idea may be a named distinction, a question, a test, an image, a procedure, or a boundary. It should remain useful when detached from the original prose. The reader need not agree with the entire article to spend the conceptual instrument elsewhere.

Useful questions:

- What could the reader apply tomorrow without quoting me?
- Does the frame generate further observations rather than merely naming mine?
- What remains productively unresolved?

## Reader-state transitions

| Function | Reader before | Reader after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hospitality | Outside the writer's frame | Standing in shared reality |
| Disturbance | Comfortable with the existing explanation | Aware that something consequential is missing |
| Negotiation | Interested but unconvinced | Able to cross without surrendering judgment |
| Delivery | Prepared for a distinction | In possession of it |
| Revaluation | Holding an abstract idea | Able to see it operate on the world |
| Inheritance | Finishing the article | Carrying a usable instrument away |

## Local and global deliveries

A longer piece may contain several deliveries. Each resolves a local tension while increasing the reader's capacity to receive the central one.

```text
setup -> local delivery A
             |
             v
       enlarged problem -> local delivery B
                                  |
                                  v
                            global delivery
                                  |
                                  v
                              inheritance
```

Local deliveries should accumulate rather than compete. If they can be rearranged without changing the argument, they may be observations sharing a document rather than parts of one article.

The global delivery should not invalidate every earlier idea merely to feel dramatic. It should reorganize them.

## Delivery tests

A candidate delivery is strong when most of these are true:

- **Prepared:** The reader has encountered the evidence or tension required to understand it.
- **Transformative:** It changes the category, relationship, or available action—not merely the intensity.
- **Compressed:** Removing a sentence does not reveal that the idea depended on hidden premises.
- **Portable:** The distinction remains useful outside this article.
- **Inspectable:** Its mechanism, evidence, and limits can be challenged.
- **Desirable:** The reader now wants the frame because the Missingness has become costly.
- **Residual:** It leaves language or an image worth remembering.

## Persona as a long-form instrument

Each sample family should receive an editorial persona. A persona is not a costume, demographic fiction, or collection of verbal mannerisms. It defines the social position from which the writer earns a delivery.

Each persona must specify:

1. **Relationship:** Who is the writer in relation to this reader?
2. **Promise:** What kind of value does the writer implicitly promise for continued attention?
3. **Hospitality:** How does this persona create shared ground?
4. **Persuasive basis:** What does this persona treat as persuasive or inspectable?
5. **Resistance:** What kind of objection must this persona honor?
6. **Route:** How does this persona characteristically prepare a delivery?
7. **Rhetorical latitude:** What forms of humor, intimacy, authority, or strangeness has the persona earned?
8. **Failure mode:** What counterfeit version of this persona must revision detect?

The persona governs the relationship and dramatic movement. The canonical short-form language governs the delivery once it arrives.

## Anti-formula constraints

This grammar becomes harmful when treated as a sequence of mandatory beats.

- Do not begin every article with a charming anecdote.
- Do not manufacture a hole whose consequences the reader cannot feel.
- Do not delay a clear thesis merely to simulate suspense.
- Do not include an objection only to defeat a weaker reader.
- Do not make every section end with a polished short-form delivery.
- Do not force revaluation into optimism or inheritance into advice.
- Do not confuse a surprising phrase with a transformed frame.
- Do not close after the article has already found its proper boundary.

Hospitality may be confrontation. Negotiation may be a worked example. Delivery may occur in the opening and spend the rest of the essay under pressure. Inheritance may be an unresolved question. The order serves the relationship; the relationship does not serve the diagram.

## Revision passes

### The missingness pass

State, privately and without ornament, the reality in which this article does not exist. What remains hard to perceive, decide, or do? If the answer is trivial, the article may not yet be necessary.

### The reader pass

Identify the reader's starting frame, reasonable resistance, and desired inheritance. Remove explanations addressed only to an imaginary fool or an imaginary expert.

### The structure pass

Mark hospitality, disturbance, negotiation, local deliveries, global delivery, revaluation, and inheritance. Diagnose missing functions and duplicated ones. Do not add headings merely to make the map complete.

### The delivery pass

Extract every sentence behaving as a delivery. Test each against the canonical short-form prompt. Remove counterfeit profundity, repeated turns, and explanatory sentences added after a clean stop.

### The mechanism pass

For every metaphor or institutional claim, ask what physically, technically, or socially occurs. Add the missing actor, boundary, action, or consequence.

### The desire pass

Ask whether the reader has acquired a use for the central idea before receiving it. Strengthen the consequential Missingness rather than advertising the conclusion.

### The residue pass

After the draft is forgotten, what remains: a distinction, image, question, or procedure? Preserve that residue. Cut decorative language competing with it.

## Evaluation sentence

The most useful verdict for a long-form draft is:

> This article would become more effective if the reader could feel **[the consequential missingness]** before receiving **[the delivery]**, and could leave with **[the inheritance]**.

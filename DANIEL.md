# Who is Daniel?

Most repositories can treat their author as implementation detail. An ontology cannot do that so easily.

Organon selects distinctions, names recurring failures, and decides which definitions must precede others. Those choices did not descend from an institution. They emerged from the work and writing of Daniel Rodriguez, then became more explicit through sustained argument with AI systems and, more recently, a proof assistant. This document states that position so readers can evaluate the instrument together with the perspective that produced it.

## The short biography

Daniel Rodriguez is a software engineer and the founder and principal engineer of [Idolum](https://github.com/idolum-ai). He has spent more than fourteen years building developer platforms, cloud SDKs, and AI systems across Microsoft, Anaconda, venture-backed startups, and independent open-source work.

At Microsoft, he led the design of `@azure/identity` and prototyped the cross-language record-and-replay approach later released as the Azure SDK Test Proxy. At Anaconda, he joined as a founding engineer for its AI product line and later helped grow the AI Engineering organization while working on Jupyter AI Assistant, privacy controls, and agent infrastructure. His current work at Idolum concerns governed agent execution: runtimes, isolation boundaries, operational evidence, and the question of what must remain outside an agent's own account of its actions.

The biography explains where some of Organon's vocabulary comes from. It does not certify the ontology.

## The recurring problem

Daniel's work repeatedly crosses a boundary that software organizations prefer to leave implicit: a technical system becomes an institutional system as soon as its outputs carry authority.

An authentication library does not merely move tokens; it encodes who may act as whom. An AI assistant does not merely produce text; it changes which claims an organization is prepared to treat as work. An agent runtime does not merely invoke tools; it participates in permissions, obligations, evidence, and completion. The technical mechanism remains real. It is simply no longer the whole mechanism.

Organon is an attempt to hold both layers in one dependency chain.

## Why the author belongs in the repository

Organon is intended to help review documentation across Idolum projects. That creates a predictable danger: Daniel is both the author of the comparison surface and a maintainer of the systems being compared. A definition can become a way of winning an argument by controlling its vocabulary.

Naming the author does not remove that conflict. It makes the conflict inspectable.

Project mechanisms remain primary evidence about what a project does. Project maintainers retain authority over their repositories. When Organon and a project disagree, the disagreement should be recorded as one of three things: a defect in the project documentation, a project-specific distinction Organon does not yet represent, or a defect in Organon. The ontology does not win by being the ontology.

## Authorship in the presence of AI

AI systems have contributed extensively to Organon's wording, criticism, organization, and Lean formalization. Daniel supplies the originating commitments, corrects the system when it departs from them, and decides which formulations become binding. The AI can discover inconsistencies and propose repairs. Lean can reject an encoding that does not type-check. Neither process independently establishes that the ontology is philosophically true.

The authorship boundary is therefore explicit:

- Daniel is responsible for adopting the commitments.
- AI-generated language is a proposal until Daniel adopts it.
- A successful Lean build is Evidence about the formal artifact, not authorship and not metaphysical proof.
- Future contributors should be credited for the distinctions, counterexamples, and corrections they introduce.

This is not an attempt to recover a fiction of solitary authorship. It is an attempt to keep collaborative authorship from becoming anonymous authority.
